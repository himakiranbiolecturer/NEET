01 · Principles

Two foundations of modern biotechnology

Genetic engineering

Alter and transfer genetic material

DNA is cut, recombined and introduced into a host so that a selected gene can be maintained, copied or expressed.

Bioprocess engineering

Grow the desired system at scale

Sterile, controlled processes support large populations of selected cells for reliable production of useful molecules.

Why an origin matters

A DNA insert cannot be copied reliably unless it is integrated into replicating DNA or linked to a vector with an origin of replication.

02 · Molecular toolkit

Cut, copy, join and modify DNA

Restriction endonuclease

Recognises a particular DNA sequence, often palindromic, and cuts within the molecule.

Exonuclease

Removes nucleotides from DNA ends rather than cutting at an internal recognition site.

DNA ligase

Seals phosphodiester bonds, joining compatible DNA fragments into a continuous molecule.

DNA polymerase

Synthesises new DNA using a template and a primer; heat-stable polymerase enables PCR cycling.

Lysozyme / cellulase / chitinase

Break bacterial, plant or fungal cell coverings respectively during DNA isolation.

Protease and RNase

Remove protein and RNA contaminants from a DNA preparation.

Name code

EcoRI: E from genus, co from species, R from strain and I indicates the first enzyme isolated from that strain.

05 · DNA delivery

Make host cells competent

Bacteria

Chemical competence

Cells are treated so DNA can cross the membrane; a brief physical stimulus assists uptake.

Animal cell

Microinjection

DNA can be delivered directly into a cell nucleus using specialised equipment.

Plant cell

Biolistics

DNA-coated microscopic particles can be propelled into target cells.

Vector-mediated

Disarmed pathogen

Pathogens modified to remove disease-causing ability can carry selected DNA into host cells.

These are conceptual NEET descriptions, not laboratory protocols. Recombinant-DNA work requires trained supervision, suitable facilities and biosafety controls.

08 · Gel electrophoresis

Separate DNA fragments by size

Why DNA moves: its phosphate backbone is negatively charged, so fragments migrate toward the positive electrode in an electric field.

Why size matters: smaller fragments move more easily through agarose pores and travel farther.

Visualisation

Separated DNA is stained and viewed under suitable illumination; visible bands correspond to groups of similarly sized fragments.

Elution

A selected band is cut from the gel and its DNA is recovered for the next step.

10 · Scale-up

Bioreactors optimise product formation

Stirred-tank design

Mixing distributes nutrients, cells, oxygen and heat; spargers can deliver sterile air.

Controlled conditions

Systems monitor temperature, pH, oxygen, foam, agitation and nutrient delivery.

Sampling ports

Small samples allow monitoring of cell growth and product formation without disrupting the process.

Downstream processing

After biosynthesis, the product is separated, purified, formulated and quality-tested. Purity requirements depend on its intended use.

12 · Rapid glossary

Last-minute definitions

Recombinant DNA

A DNA molecule formed by joining sequences from different sources.

Restriction site

A specific DNA sequence recognised by a restriction endonuclease.

Sticky end

A short single-stranded overhang that can base-pair with a complementary end.

Vector

A replicating DNA vehicle used to carry an inserted sequence into a host.

Transformant

A host cell that has taken up introduced DNA.

PCR

An in-vitro method that exponentially amplifies a selected DNA region.

Elution

Recovery of a selected DNA fragment from a gel.

Downstream processing

Product recovery, purification, formulation and quality control after biosynthesis.
