01 · Population level

Ecology links individuals to organised groups

A population consists of individuals of the same species occupying a defined area, using similar resources and potentially reproducing with one another.

Why population?

Natural selection changes trait frequencies across populations, while individuals directly encounter environmental conditions.

Asexual organisms

Groups produced asexually are also treated as populations for ecological analysis.

Examples

Lotus plants in a pond, cormorants in a wetland, bacteria in a culture and teak trees in a forest tract.

02 · Population attributes

Properties that an individual cannot possess

Natality

Per-capita addition of new individuals by reproduction during a specified interval.

Mortality

Per-capita loss through death during a specified interval.

Sex ratio

Relative proportions of males and females in a population.

Age structure

Proportions in pre-reproductive, reproductive and post-reproductive age groups.

Rate versus count

Eight births in a population of 20 correspond to a birth rate of 8/20 = 0.4 per individual for that interval—not simply “8”.

05 · Growth-curve studio

Compare exponential and logistic growth

Exponential: J-shaped

With unlimited resources, dN/dt = rN and Nt = N₀ert.

Logistic: S-shaped

With finite resources, dN/dt = rN[(K − N)/K]. Growth slows as N approaches K.

Maximum logistic growth

The absolute growth rate rN(1 − N/K) is highest at N = K/2.

Do not confuse

r is the intrinsic rate of natural increase; K is the carrying capacity of the habitat.

07 · Life-history variation

Different reproductive strategies can maximise fitness

Breed once

Pacific salmon and bamboo invest in a single major reproductive episode.

Breed repeatedly

Most birds and mammals reproduce multiple times across their lives.

Many small offspring

Oysters and many pelagic fishes produce large numbers with low investment per offspring.

Few large offspring

Birds and mammals generally invest more resources in each of fewer offspring.

No universally best strategy

Life-history traits evolve under the biotic and abiotic constraints of a species’ habitat.

11 · Rapid glossary

Last-minute definitions

Population

Individuals of one species occupying a defined area and sharing ecological resources.

Natality

Addition of new individuals through reproduction during a specified interval.

Immigration

Entry of individuals of the same species from another area.

Intrinsic rate (r)

Per-capita potential rate of natural increase under stated conditions.

Carrying capacity (K)

Population size that a habitat can support sustainably under prevailing conditions.

Competitive exclusion

Complete competitors cannot coexist indefinitely when the same limiting resource is contested.

Commensalism

One species benefits while the other is neither significantly helped nor harmed.

Mutualism

An interspecific relationship in which both partners benefit.
