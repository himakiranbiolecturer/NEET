01 · Core idea

More than absence of disease

Reproductive health means total well-being in the physical, emotional, behavioural and social dimensions of reproduction. A reproductively healthy society supports informed, responsible and safe choices.

Physical

Healthy function

Normal reproductive organs and processes, timely care and disease prevention.

Emotional & behavioural

Informed decisions

Accurate knowledge, responsible behaviour and freedom from myths or coercion.

Social

Supportive systems

Accessible care, awareness, equality and protection from harmful practices.

NCERT frame

India was among the first countries to begin national programmes for family planning and later broadened these into reproductive and child healthcare initiatives.

04 · High-yield mechanisms

Three classes of IUDs

Non-medicated

Lippes loop

Enhances phagocytosis of sperm within the uterus.

Copper-releasing

CuT, Cu7, Multiload 375

Copper ions suppress sperm motility and fertilising capacity; IUD presence also promotes phagocytosis.

Hormone-releasing

Progestasert, LNG-20

Makes the uterus less suitable for implantation and the cervix less receptive to sperm.

Why IUDs are popular

They are inserted by trained healthcare professionals and are useful for people seeking spacing between pregnancies.

05 · MTP and diagnostic ethics

Separate treatment, contraception and diagnosis

MTP

Medical termination of pregnancy

Intentional termination of a pregnancy under medical care. It is not a routine contraceptive method. NCERT emphasises that earlier procedures are medically safer than later ones.

Indications and legal limits depend on current law and clinical assessment; this page does not provide individual medical guidance.

Amniocentesis

Diagnostic technique

Fetal cells and dissolved substances in amniotic fluid can help identify certain genetic or developmental conditions.

Its misuse for prenatal sex determination led to legal prohibition of that misuse.

Exam trap

MTP terminates an established pregnancy; contraception aims to prevent pregnancy. Amniocentesis has valid diagnostic uses, but sex-selection misuse is prohibited.

Safe-care principle

Medical procedures should be performed only within the law by qualified professionals in approved healthcare settings.

06 · Sexually transmitted infections

Recognise, prevent, treat early

Some infections may produce few early symptoms. Untreated infection can lead to pelvic inflammatory disease, infertility, pregnancy complications or other long-term harm.

Bacterial

Gonorrhoea

Can often be treated when diagnosed promptly; delayed care increases complications.

Bacterial

Syphilis

Requires professional diagnosis and complete treatment.

Bacterial

Chlamydiasis

May be asymptomatic and can affect reproductive health if untreated.

Viral

Genital herpes

Viral infection; early medical evaluation supports management and prevention of spread.

Viral

Hepatitis B

Can spread through sexual contact and infected body fluids; vaccination is an important preventive measure.

Viral

HIV infection

Targets the immune system. Testing, medical care and prevention are essential.

Prevention and care: accurate education, barrier protection such as condoms, avoiding unsafe exposure, vaccination where available, early testing, professional medical advice and completion of prescribed treatment.
07 · Infertility

A couple-level health issue

Infertility can result from physical, congenital, disease-related, medicine-related, immunological or psychological factors involving either partner. Evaluation should avoid blame and involve qualified specialists.

Diagnosis

Find the cause

Clinical history and appropriate investigations help identify whether treatment or assisted reproduction may help.

Treatment

Cause-specific care

Some problems can be corrected with medicines, counselling or procedures.

Assistance

ART options

Assisted reproductive technologies help by handling gametes or early embryos in carefully controlled clinical settings.

11 · Rapid glossary

Last-minute definitions

Contraception

Intentional prevention of pregnancy using behavioural, barrier, intrauterine, hormonal or surgical methods.

MTP

Medical termination of an established pregnancy under qualified care and applicable law.

STI

An infection that may be transmitted through sexual contact; some can also spread by other routes.

Infertility

Difficulty in achieving pregnancy despite regular attempts, requiring couple-based medical evaluation.

IVF

Fertilisation of gametes outside the body under laboratory conditions.

ZIFT

Transfer of a zygote or early embryo up to eight blastomeres into a fallopian tube.

IUT

Transfer of an embryo with more than eight blastomeres into the uterus.

ICSI

Direct injection of one sperm into an ovum.
