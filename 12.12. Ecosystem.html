12.1 · Big picture

Ecosystem structure & function

An ecosystem is a functional unit in which organisms interact with one another and with the physical environment. Its two broad structural components are biotic and abiotic.

Consumers

Decomposers

Bacteria & fungi

Act on dead matter and return inorganic nutrients to the environment.

12.2 · Organic matter formation

Productivity

Productivity is the rate of biomass production. It is expressed per unit area per unit time, often as mass or energy.

Gross primary productivity (GPP)

Total rate at which producers capture energy and form organic matter through photosynthesis.

Net primary productivity (NPP)

Biomass remaining after producer respiration; it is available to heterotrophs.

Secondary productivity

Rate of formation of new organic matter by consumers.

GPP−Respiration (R)=NPP
NEET trapNPP is not the energy initially trapped by plants. It is what remains after autotrophic respiration is deducted from GPP.

12.3 · Detritus pathway

Decomposition

Decomposition breaks complex detritus into simpler inorganic substances. Important raw material includes dead plant matter, animal remains and faecal matter.

FragmentationDetritivores break detritus into smaller particles.
→
LeachingWater-soluble nutrients move downward into soil layers.
→
CatabolismMicrobial enzymes convert complex matter into simpler compounds.
→
HumificationDark, resistant humus accumulates in soil.
→
MineralisationMicrobes release inorganic nutrients from organic matter.

Faster decomposition

Warm, moist and well-aerated conditions; detritus rich in nitrogen and water-soluble substances.

Slower decomposition

Low temperature, anaerobic conditions or detritus rich in lignin and chitin.

Humus ≠ mineralisationHumus is highly resistant to microbial action and acts as a nutrient reservoir. Mineralisation is the release of inorganic nutrients.

12.4 · Transfer through trophic levels

Energy flow

The Sun is the ultimate energy source for most ecosystems. Less than half of incident solar radiation is photosynthetically active radiation (PAR), and plants capture only a small fraction of PAR.

PAR

Photosynthetically active radiation forms less than 50% of incident solar radiation.

Plant capture

Green plants capture about 2–10% of PAR and convert it to chemical energy.

Direction

Energy flow is unidirectional: Sun → producers → consumers. Energy does not cycle back.

Lindeman's principle

The 10% law

Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next; most is used in life processes and dissipated as heat.

⚡ Trophic energy model

Exam logicApply 10% successively. If producers hold 10,000 J, primary consumers receive about 1,000 J, secondary consumers 100 J and tertiary consumers 10 J.
12.5 · Quantitative representation

Ecological pyramids

Each bar represents a trophic level. Producers form the base and successive consumers occupy higher levels. Pyramids can express number, biomass or energy.

Pyramid of number

PyramidTypical shapeImportant exception / reason
NumberOften uprightCan be inverted, e.g. one tree supports many herbivorous insects.
BiomassUpright in many terrestrial systemsOften inverted in aquatic systems because phytoplankton have low standing crop but rapid turnover.
EnergyAlways uprightEnergy is lost at each transfer and cannot increase toward higher trophic levels.
LimitationsSimple pyramids assume one food chain, do not comfortably place species occupying multiple trophic levels, and usually exclude saprophytes/decomposers despite their vital role.
Last-minute recall

NEET quick revision

GPP − R = NPP

NPP is available to heterotrophs.

Warm + moist

Favour decomposition; anaerobic conditions inhibit it.

Energy is one-way

Nutrients cycle; energy flows and is dissipated.

Energy pyramid

Always upright; number and biomass may invert.

High-yield distinctions

PairDistinction
Standing crop vs standing stateStanding crop is living biomass/number at a trophic level; standing state is the amount of nutrients in soil or water at a given time.
Detritivore vs decomposerDetritivores fragment detritus; microbial decomposers act enzymatically and mineralise organic matter.
Primary vs secondary productivityPrimary productivity belongs to producers; secondary productivity is new organic matter formed by consumers.
Food chain vs food webA chain shows one pathway; a web shows interconnected feeding pathways.
