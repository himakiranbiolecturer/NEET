01 · DNA

Structure explains copying

Backbone

Alternating deoxyribose and phosphate joined by 3′–5′ phosphodiester bonds.

Pairing

A–T uses two hydrogen bonds; G–C uses three. A purine pairs with a pyrimidine.

Geometry

Right-handed, antiparallel helix; 0.34 nm between base pairs, about 10 bp per turn, pitch 3.4 nm.

Chargaff’s rule

In double-stranded DNA, A = T and G = C. Complementarity allows each parental strand to guide synthesis of a new strand.

02 · Search for genetic material

Three experiments, one conclusion

Griffith

Transformation

Heat-killed virulent bacteria transformed living non-virulent bacteria, revealing a transferable principle.

Avery–MacLeod–McCarty

DNA identified

DNase destroyed transforming activity; protease and RNase did not. DNA was the transforming principle.

Hershey–Chase

Phage evidence

³²P-labelled DNA entered bacteria; ³⁵S-labelled protein largely remained outside.

Why DNA is stable

Double-stranded repair template, deoxyribose, and thymine support chemical stability.

RNA world idea

RNA can store information, act as adaptor/structural material and catalyse reactions; it may have preceded DNA-protein systems.

04 · Replication

Semi-conservative and direction-sensitive

1. UnwindHelicase separates strands at the replication fork.
2. PrimePrimase supplies short RNA primers with free 3′-OH.
3. ExtendDNA polymerase synthesises only 5′→3′.
4. JoinLigase connects Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand.

Leading strand

Synthesised continuously toward the moving fork.

Lagging strand

Synthesised discontinuously away from the fork as Okazaki fragments.

Meselson–Stahl

¹⁵N/¹⁴N density-gradient results supported semi-conservative replication: every daughter DNA receives one parental and one new strand.

Direction trap

Polymerase reads template 3′→5′ while building the new strand 5′→3′.

09 · Human Genome Project

Read the whole genome

~3.3 × 10⁹bp haploid genome
<2%codes for proteins
99.9%human sequence similarity
Goals

Identify genes, determine base sequence, store data, improve analysis tools, transfer technology and consider ethical/legal/social issues.

Approaches

Expressed sequence tags focused on expressed genes; sequence annotation examined the entire genome and assigned functions.

Chromosome 1

Has the most genes; Y chromosome has the fewest among human chromosomes.

Variation

Single-nucleotide polymorphisms and other variants help explain individuality, disease susceptibility and ancestry.

10 · DNA fingerprinting

Variation becomes an identity pattern

DNA isolation
→

→

Band-pattern comparison

DNA fingerprinting exploits polymorphism in repetitive DNA, especially variable-number tandem repeats. Patterns are highly individual-specific except in identical twins and can support forensic, parentage and population studies.

Do not confuse

A DNA fingerprint compares polymorphic sequence patterns; it is not the same as sequencing the entire genome.

12 · Rapid glossary

Last-minute definitions

Antiparallel

Two DNA strands run in opposite directions: 5′→3′ and 3′→5′.

Replication fork

Y-shaped region where parental DNA separates and new strands are synthesised.

Transcription unit

DNA region containing promoter, structural gene and terminator.

Codon

Three-base mRNA unit that specifies an amino acid or termination.

Anticodon

Three-base tRNA sequence complementary to an mRNA codon.

Operon

Coordinately regulated group of genes with shared control elements.

Polymorphism

Heritable DNA-sequence variation present in a population.

VNTR

Variable-number tandem repeat used in DNA fingerprinting.
