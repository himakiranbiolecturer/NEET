01 · Foundations

Health is dynamic well-being

Health depends on biological, psychological and social factors. Disease disrupts normal structure or function and may be infectious or non-infectious.

Personal factors

Genetic constitution, nutrition, sleep, hygiene, immunity and behaviour influence health.

Community factors

Clean water, sanitation, waste management, vector control and access to healthcare shape disease risk.

Disease types

Infectious diseases are caused by transmissible agents; non-infectious diseases include genetic, degenerative, metabolic and malignant conditions.

NEET habit

For every infectious disease, connect five items: causative agent → transmission → target/major symptom → diagnostic clue → prevention.

03 · Malaria

Follow Plasmodium through two hosts

1Female Anopheles injects sporozoites
2Sporozoites enter liver cells
3Parasites invade red blood cells
4RBC rupture releases haemozoin and causes fever cycles
5Mosquito ingests gametocytes; sexual phase occurs in mosquito
Human host

Asexual multiplication occurs in liver cells and red blood cells. The infective stage entering humans is the sporozoite.

Mosquito host

Sexual reproduction occurs in the female Anopheles; therefore, the mosquito is the definitive host.

Why chills recur

Synchronous rupture of infected red blood cells releases parasites and haemozoin, producing periodic bouts of fever and chills.

Vector trap

Only the female Anopheles transmits human malaria; “mosquito” alone is incomplete in many NEET options.

04 · Immune system

Layered defence: rapid first, specific next

PhysicalSkin and mucus-lined epithelia block entry.
PhysiologicalStomach acid, saliva and tears inhibit microbes.
CellularPhagocytes and natural killer cells attack invaders or abnormal cells.
CytokineInterferons help protect non-infected cells during viral infection.

B lymphocytes

Differentiate into plasma cells that secrete antibodies and into memory B cells.

T lymphocytes

Helper T cells coordinate responses; cytotoxic T cells destroy infected or abnormal host cells.

Active immunity

The person’s immune system responds to antigen. It develops more slowly but produces memory.

Passive immunity

Ready-made antibodies provide immediate, temporary protection without generating immunological memory.

Vaccination

Safe antigenic material or instructions expose the immune system without causing the target disease, generating memory cells and a stronger later response.

Passive protection

Antibodies may cross the placenta or occur in colostrum; therapeutic antibody preparations can also give immediate short-lived protection.

06 · Dysregulated immunity

When defence overreacts or misidentifies self

Allergy

An exaggerated immune response to otherwise harmless environmental antigens. IgE and mediators such as histamine are central textbook associations.

Autoimmunity

Loss of self-tolerance allows immune responses against the body’s own molecules, cells or tissues.

Immunodeficiency

One or more immune components are absent or impaired, increasing susceptibility to infection.

Health note: Severe breathing difficulty, swelling, faintness or rapidly worsening allergic symptoms require urgent medical help. The page teaches concepts and is not a diagnostic guide.
07 · HIV and AIDS

New virions form; CD4 helper T-cell function declines
Transmission

Occurs through specific body-fluid exposures, including unprotected sexual contact, contaminated blood or shared injection equipment, and parent-to-child transmission.

Not transmitted by

Ordinary social contact such as touching, sharing a classroom, hugging or mosquito bites does not spread HIV.

Effect

Loss of helper T-cell function impairs both antibody-mediated and cell-mediated responses, allowing opportunistic infections and certain cancers.

Prevention and care

Screened blood, sterile equipment, barrier protection, testing and effective antiretroviral therapy are key public-health measures. Stigma has no place in care.

HIV ≠ AIDS

HIV is the virus. AIDS is the advanced clinical syndrome caused by severe immune damage. Treatment can suppress the virus and prevent progression.

08 · Cancer biology

Loss of growth control and tissue boundaries

Transformation

Normal cells accumulate changes that disrupt cell-cycle control, DNA repair or programmed cell death.

Invasion

Malignant cells invade nearby tissues; unlike benign tumours, they are not confined to their original site.

Metastasis

Cancer cells spread to distant sites through blood or lymph and establish secondary tumours.

CategoryExamples or principle
CarcinogensPhysical agents such as ionising/UV radiation, chemical agents, and some oncogenic viruses can increase risk.
DetectionImaging may locate a mass; biopsy and histopathology examine suspicious tissue; selected molecular tests detect markers.
TreatmentSurgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy or immunotherapy may be used alone or in combinations decided by specialists.
Contact inhibition

Normal cells generally stop dividing when they contact neighbours. Many cancer cells lose this growth-regulating behaviour.

09 · Prevention

Substance misuse: biology, risk and support

NCERT connects adolescence, peer pressure, dependence and long-term health effects. Prevention focuses on informed choices and timely support.

Opioids

Act through opioid receptors and can dangerously depress body functions. Medical use requires professional supervision.

Cannabinoids

Act on cannabinoid receptors and can alter coordination, perception, memory and judgement.

Cocaine

A stimulant that interferes with dopamine transport and can severely affect the cardiovascular and nervous systems.

Protective actions

Learn accurate information, resist pressure, build healthy coping skills, and seek help early from a trusted adult, counsellor or qualified health professional.

No stigma

Dependence is a health condition—not a moral label. Support, professional care and a safe environment improve recovery.

11 · Rapid glossary

Last-minute definitions

Pathogen

A biological agent capable of causing disease in a suitable host.

Vector

An organism that transfers a pathogen between hosts.

Antigen

A molecule recognised specifically by immune receptors or antibodies.

Antibody

An immunoglobulin secreted by plasma cells that binds a specific antigen.

Immunological memory

Enhanced speed and strength of response after re-exposure to the same antigen.

Retrovirus

An RNA virus that uses reverse transcriptase to form a DNA intermediate.

Metastasis

Spread of malignant cells from a primary tumour to distant sites.

Dependence

A condition in which repeated substance use produces strong psychological or physical need and impaired control.
