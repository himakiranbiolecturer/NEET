Why pea?

Clear contrasting traits, short generation time, many offspring, naturally self-pollinating flowers and easy artificial crossing.

True breeding

Repeated self-pollination produces stable expression of a trait across generations.

Mendel’s strategy

Study one or two traits at a time, count large progeny sets and analyse ratios mathematically.

02 · Mendel’s laws

Three rules, three questions

Expression

Dominance

In a heterozygote, one allele may express while the other remains masked.

Gamete formation

Segregation

The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation; a gamete receives only one.

Two gene pairs

Independent assortment

Alleles of different genes assort independently when the genes are unlinked.

Monohybrid F₂

3 : 1 phenotype

Genotype ratio = 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa under complete dominance.

Dihybrid F₂

9 : 3 : 3 : 1

Expected when two gene pairs assort independently with complete dominance.

04 · Beyond simple dominance

Same segregation, different phenotype rules

Key insight

Incomplete dominance does not break segregation. In an F₂ cross, genotype and phenotype ratios both become 1:2:1 because the heterozygote has a distinct phenotype.

06 · Two genes

Independent assortment versus linkage

Unlinked genes

AaBb forms four gamete types

ABAbaBab

Equal gamete frequencies and complete dominance can produce the classic 9:3:3:1 F₂ ratio.

Linked genes

Parental combinations predominate

Genes on the same chromosome tend to travel together. Crossing over produces recombinants; the farther apart the genes, the greater the usual recombination frequency.

Morgan’s contribution

Drosophila crosses established linkage and recombination as chromosome-based explanations for departures from independent assortment.

07 · Chromosomal theory

Genes and chromosomes behave in parallel

Alleles occur in pairs
↔
Homologous chromosomes occur in pairs
→
Pairs separate in meiosis
→
Gametes receive one member

Sutton and Boveri proposed that chromosome movement during meiosis provides the physical basis for Mendel’s factors. Fertilisation restores chromosome pairs, just as it restores allele pairs.

08 · Sex determination

Compare chromosome systems

Humans & many insects

XX–XY

Female XX, male XY. The male is heterogametic and produces X- and Y-bearing sperm.

Birds

ZZ–ZW

Male ZZ, female ZW. The female is heterogametic and produces Z- and W-bearing ova.

Honey bee

Haplodiploidy

Fertilised diploid eggs develop into females; unfertilised haploid eggs develop into males.

Classic trap

In humans, the sperm determines XX or XY. In birds, the ovum determines ZZ or ZW. In honey bees, ploidy depends on fertilisation.

09 · Pedigree logic

Use inheritance clues, not guesswork

Pedigree symbols

Square = male; circle = female; filled symbol = affected; horizontal line = mating; vertical line = descent.

Start with exclusions

Male-to-male transmission excludes X-linkage. An affected daughter with unaffected parents strongly suggests autosomal recessive inheritance in standard pedigree assumptions.

12 · Rapid glossary

Last-minute definitions

Allele

An alternative form of a gene at the same locus on homologous chromosomes.

Homozygous

Having identical alleles for a gene.

Heterozygous

Having different alleles for a gene.

Test cross

Cross of an individual with dominant phenotype to a homozygous recessive individual.

Codominance

Both alleles express in a heterozygote, as with Iᴬ and Iᴮ.

Linkage

Tendency of genes on the same chromosome to be inherited together.

Recombination

Formation of allele combinations different from parental combinations.

Nondisjunction

Failure of chromosomes to separate normally during cell division.
