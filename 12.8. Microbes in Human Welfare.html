01 · Household products

Fermentation begins close to home

Curd

Lactic acid bacteria

LAB convert milk sugar to lactic acid, coagulating and partially digesting milk proteins. They also enrich curd nutritionally.

Dough and batter

Microbial leavening

Microbial carbon dioxide expands dough and fermented batter, producing a soft, porous texture.

Traditional foods

Regional fermentations

Microbial communities create characteristic flavours and textures in many traditional foods and beverages.

NCERT clue

Lactobacillus and other LAB improve curd formation; yeast such as Saccharomyces cerevisiae is central to dough fermentation and large-scale fermentations.

02 · Microbe–product explorer

Match the organism to its useful output

Fermenters

Large vessels provide controlled temperature, pH, mixing, aeration and nutrient conditions for microbial growth and product recovery.

Antibiotics

Microbial compounds can inhibit or kill other microbes. Penicillin’s discovery and development transformed treatment of bacterial infections.

This is a biology overview of industrial fermentation, not a production procedure. Some fermentation products are age-restricted and must be treated within applicable health and legal rules.

Primary treatment

Physical processes remove floating debris and settleable solids. The settled material is primary sludge.

Secondary treatment

Aerobic microbial flocs consume organic matter, sharply reducing biochemical oxygen demand.

Activated sludge

Some settled microbial biomass returns as inoculum; the remainder enters anaerobic digesters.

BOD meaning

Biochemical oxygen demand estimates oxygen required by microbes to oxidise organic matter. Higher BOD indicates greater organic pollution.

Methanogens

Archaea such as Methanobacterium produce methane during anaerobic digestion.

Rumen link

Methanogens occur in the rumen of cattle and help process cellulose-rich feed in the microbial community.

Resource recovery

Anaerobic treatment stabilises organic waste while yielding combustible biogas and nutrient-containing slurry.

06 · Biocontrol

Use ecological relationships against pests

Bacterium

Bacillus thuringiensis

Produces insecticidal proteins; formulations or Bt genes can protect crops against susceptible insect larvae.

Fungus

Trichoderma

Free-living fungi used against several plant pathogens, especially in root ecosystems.

Virus

Baculoviruses

Nucleopolyhedroviruses are species-specific biocontrol agents with limited effects on plants, mammals, birds and many beneficial insects.

Predator

Ladybird beetle

Predatory beetles help control aphid populations.

Predator

Dragonfly

Dragonflies consume mosquitoes and contribute to biological control.

IPM principle

Integrated pest management combines monitoring and compatible control methods, reducing unnecessary reliance on broad-spectrum chemicals.

07 · Biofertilisers

Improve nutrient availability biologically

Rhizobium

Forms symbiotic root nodules in legumes and fixes atmospheric nitrogen.

Free-living bacteria

Azotobacter and Azospirillum enrich soil nitrogen without legume nodules.

Cyanobacteria

Anabaena, Nostoc and related forms add nitrogen and organic matter, especially in paddy fields.

Mycorrhiza

Fungi such as Glomus associate with roots and improve phosphorus and water uptake.

Plant benefits

Mycorrhizal plants may show improved drought tolerance, root-borne pathogen resistance and overall growth.

Do not confuse

Biofertilisers improve nutrient availability; biocontrol agents suppress pests or pathogens. A microbe may have more than one ecological role, but the terms are not synonyms.

09 · Rapid glossary

Last-minute definitions

Fermentation

Microbial conversion of substrates into useful products under controlled metabolic conditions.

Antibiotic

A microbial product or derivative that inhibits or kills susceptible microorganisms.

Floc

A mesh-like aggregate of bacteria associated with fungal filaments in aeration tanks.

BOD

Oxygen needed by microbes to oxidise organic matter in a water sample.

Activated sludge

Settled microbial flocs from secondary treatment, partly recycled as inoculum.

Methanogen

An anaerobic archaeon that produces methane.

Biocontrol

Use of living organisms or their products to suppress pests and pathogens.

Biofertiliser

A preparation of beneficial organisms that improves nutrient availability to plants.
