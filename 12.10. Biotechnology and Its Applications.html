02 · Plant biotechnology

Tissue culture uses cellular totipotency

Totipotency

The genetic capacity of a suitable plant cell or explant to regenerate an entire plant under appropriate culture conditions.

Micropropagation

Rapid production of many genetically similar plants. Plants derived from tissue culture are called somaclones.

Meristem culture

Apical or axillary meristems can be cultured to obtain healthy plants from some virus-infected stock.

Somatic hybridisation

Cell walls are removed to obtain protoplasts; protoplasts of different varieties can be fused and regenerated.

Pomato example

Tomato and potato protoplast fusion produced a somatic hybrid, although it did not combine all desired commercial traits.

NEET distinction

A somaclone arises by tissue culture; a somatic hybrid arises by fusion of protoplasts from different sources.

03 · Genetically modified crops

A transferred gene can supply a chosen trait

Abiotic stress tolerance

Traits may improve tolerance to drought, salinity, heat or cold.

Pest resistance

Targeted protection can reduce dependence on chemical pesticides.

Post-harvest performance

Modification may reduce losses during storage and distribution.

Mineral-use efficiency

More efficient nutrient use can slow depletion of soil fertility.

Nutrition

Golden rice is the textbook example of rice enriched for provitamin A-related nutrition.

Tailor-made resources

Plants can be engineered as sources of starches, fuels or pharmaceutical molecules.

04 · Bt action simulator

Why Bt is safe to its producer but active in a susceptible insect

Inactive protoxin crystal
Alkaline-gut activation
Receptor binding
Pore formation

Source

Bacillus thuringiensis produces crystalline insecticidal proteins as inactive protoxins.

Specificity

Different cry genes target different insect groups. Gene choice depends on crop and pest.

Examples

cryIAc and cryIIAb target cotton bollworms; cryIAb targets corn borer in the NCERT account.

Common trap

Bt toxin is made as an inactive protoxin in the bacterium. Activation occurs after ingestion by a susceptible insect under suitable gut conditions.

05 · RNA interference

→
Sequence-specific mRNA silencing
→
Target protein not produced

Cellular principle

RNAi is a sequence-specific defence mechanism in eukaryotes. Complementary double-stranded RNA triggers silencing of a matching mRNA.

NCERT example

A transgenic tobacco plant produced RNA matching a gene of the root-knot nematode Meloidogyne incognita, preventing successful parasitism.

06 · Recombinant insulin builder

Remove the C-peptide to reveal mature insulin

A chain—S—S—C-peptide—S—S—B chain

Prohormone

Human insulin is initially made with an extra connecting C-peptide, which is removed during maturation.

Recombinant production

In the classic Eli Lilly strategy, A and B chains were produced separately in E. coli and joined by disulfide bonds.

This is an educational molecular model. Insulin treatment and dosing require professional medical supervision.
07 · Gene therapy

Supply a functional gene to compensate for a defect

Goal

Introduce a functional gene into selected cells so it can compensate for a non-functional gene.

ADA deficiency example

Functional ADA cDNA was introduced into lymphocytes outside the body, and corrected cells were returned to the patient.

Why repeat treatment?

Corrected lymphocytes are not permanently self-renewing, so the textbook somatic-cell approach may require periodic reinfusion.

Potentially longer-lasting correction

Introducing the functional gene into appropriate early progenitor cells could provide a more durable effect than modifying short-lived lymphocytes.

08 · Molecular diagnosis selector

Detect infection or mutation before symptoms become obvious

PCR

Amplifies target nucleic acid.

ELISA

Uses antigen–antibody recognition.

DNA probe

Hybridises with a complementary sequence.

Why molecular diagnosis is early

Amplification and specific molecular binding can reveal very low pathogen load or a genetic change before a large physiological effect appears.

09 · Transgenic animals

Five major research uses

Normal physiology

Observe how a gene is regulated and how its product influences growth or development.

Disease models

Reproduce selected features of human disorders to study mechanisms and test interventions.

Biological products

Produce useful human proteins in animal secretions after suitable genetic modification.

Vaccine safety

Evaluate safety before broader use, within regulated research programmes.

Chemical safety

Assess toxicity or sensitivity to selected compounds.

Rosie example

The textbook highlights a transgenic cow producing milk enriched with human alpha-lactalbumin.

10 · Ethics, regulation and ownership

Benefit must be balanced with safety and fairness

Biosafety

Evaluate environmental effects, food/feed safety, gene flow, non-target organisms and monitoring needs.

GEAC

NCERT identifies India’s GEAC as the body that evaluates GM research and decisions concerning release for public use.

Biopatent

A patent concerning biological material, a biological product or a biotechnological process.

Biopiracy

Commercial use of biological resources or traditional knowledge without proper authorisation, recognition or benefit-sharing.

Traditional knowledge

Documented community knowledge can help challenge inappropriate ownership claims and support fair benefit-sharing.

Evaluation, not slogans

NEET questions distinguish scientific benefit from regulatory approval. A useful trait still requires evidence-based safety assessment.

12 · Rapid glossary

Last-minute definitions

Totipotency

Capacity of a suitable plant cell or explant to regenerate a whole plant.

Somaclone

A plant produced through tissue culture and genetically similar to the source plant.

Somatic hybrid

A plant regenerated after fusion of protoplasts from different sources.

Protoxin

An inactive precursor converted into an active toxin under suitable conditions.

RNA interference

Sequence-specific silencing of mRNA triggered by complementary double-stranded RNA.

Gene therapy

Introduction of functional genetic material into cells to compensate for a defect.

Transgenic organism

An organism containing and expressing deliberately introduced genetic material.

Biopiracy

Unfair commercial use of biological resources or traditional knowledge without proper consent or benefit-sharing.
